    In general, the expertise reversal effect is associated with extraneous cognitive load that could overload working memory. It is believed that the expertise reversal effect is caused by imbalances between learner organized knowledge base and provided external instructional guidance during information processing (Kalyuga & Sweller, 2005). However, in some circumstances, the available working memory capacity of advanced learners could also be sufficient for processing complex information or redundant information and, consequently, the techniques/instructional procedures that benefit novice learners might have neutral effects on higher prior knowledge learners. Such situations could also be considered as related to the expertise reversal effect (e.g., Calisir & Gurel, 2003; Clarke et al., 2005; Lee et al., 2006; Mayer & Gallini, 1990; Pollock et al., 2002).
