1.3 The Present Study
The goal of the present study was to assess the proficiency
levels of learners in the new Duolingo path when they reached
the end of A2-level content in the Spanish and French courses
for English speakers. Unlike previous studies that examined
different language skills of Duolingo learners separately (Jiang
et al., 2020; Jiang, Rollinson, Chen, et al., 2021), the current
study evaluated learners’ proficiency in all four language skills
(reading, listening, writing, and speaking). This was done
through a comprehensive standardized test, which provided an
overalllanguageproficiencyscoretogetherwithaseparatescore
foreachskill.
In addition, this study aimed to delve into the relationships
between language proficiency results of participants and their
patterns of app usage. It is crucial to identify which
learning behaviors in the app are associated with better
learning outcomes, but current research on this topic is scarce.
Sudina and Plonsky (2023) examined learners’ frequency and
intensity of exposure and in-app behavior over six months
of learning Spanish or French on Duolingo, in relation to
language improvement. Findings indicated a correlation
between frequency of logins and learning outcomes, but not
between learning outcomes and the total learning time or the
overall number of sessions completed. Smith et al. ( 2024)
foundthatsessionaccuracyratesignificantlypredictedreading,
speaking, and overall language proficiency. There is a need for
furtherresearchtoconfirmthesecorrelations,andtoexplorethe
relationshipbetweenproficiencyandotherusagevariables.
Thepresentstudyalsoaimedtoexploretherelationshipbetween
participants’ self-efficacy and their proficiency. Self-efficacy
refers to personal beliefs in one’s capabilities. It is a construct
grounded in social cognitive theory, which emphasizes that
individuals’ beliefs about themselves influence their actions
(Bandura, 1997). In educational contexts, self-efficacy has
beenfoundtoplayavitalroleinlearners’performance( Huang,
2016;Zimmerman,2000): self-efficacycorrelatesstronglywith
actual task performance and learning in a variety of domains
(Honicke & Broadbent, 2016), with a unique effect that is not
explainedbypriorperformancealone( Talsmaetal.,2018 ). Self-
efficacy also correlates with behaviors that promote learning,
such as goal-setting, persistence, and effort (Bandura, 1999;
Carmichael & Taylor, 2005; Pajares, 1996; Raoofi et al.,
2012; Schunk, 2003; Zimmerman, 2000). Existing research
shows that language learning self-efficacy is also correlated
with second language achievement (Goetze & Driver, 2022),
and learners with high language learning self-efficacy tend to
use more learning strategies (Graham, 2022; Raoofi et al.,
2012; Wang & Sun, 2020). However, there is very limited
research on self-efficacy in the context of mobile language
learning (Babakhani & Tabatabaee-Yazdi, 2023; Rachels &
Rockinson-Szapkiw, 2018). Rachels and Rockinson-Szapkiw
(2018) found no significant self-efficacy differences between
classroom instruction and learning on Duolingo, in a group
of elementary school students learning Spanish for 12 weeks.
However, Duolingo was designed for use by adult learners,
and there is no prior research on adult Duolingo learners’ self-
efficacy.
This study was intended to be exploratory in nature. It was the
first study in which we investigated the efficacy of Duolingo’s
coursesafteritshomescreenredesign,andthefirststudywitha
morecomprehensiveproficiencytesttargetingallfourlanguage
skills. We also aimed to explore the role of multiple app
usagevariablesandtheroleofself-efficacyinlanguagelearning.
Specifically, the present study aimed to answer the following
researchquestions:
1. What proficiency levels do learners achieve at the
completion of the beginner-level content (up to the end
of A2) in the new path of Duolingo’s Spanish or French
course for English speakers? How do their proficiency
levels compare to those of learners assessed in previous
studies?
2. How do learners’ app usage variables (i.e., average
session accuracy, time spent learning, and number of
sessions completed) correlate with proficiency scores at
thecompletionofthebeginner-levelcontent?
3. What levels of self-efficacy do learners have at the
completion of the beginner-level content? How do their
levelsofself-efficacycorrelatewithproficiencyscores?

2.2.2 Proficiency Test: STAMP 4S Spanish and French Tests
The STAMP 4S Spanish and French tests were used to assess
participants’SpanishorFrenchproficiencyinthisstudy. These
tests are commercial standardized proficiency tests provided
by Avant Assessment. The term STAMP is an acronym
for Standards-Based Measurement of Proficiency, while 4S
represents the four skills assessed: reading, listening, writing,
and speaking. The STAMP 4S test is an online, computer-
adaptiveevaluationoflanguageproficiency. Proficiencyscores
are presented as STAMP levels ranging from 1 to 9, which are
alignedtotheproficiencyscaleoftheAmericanCouncilonthe
Teaching of Foreign Languages (ACTFL). Test-takers can also
receive a score of 0 or their responses can be marked as Not
Ratable (NR) in case of technical difficulties with e.g., audio
recordings for the speaking section. As illustrated in Figure3,
the STAMP scale of 1-9 aligns with nine ACTFL sublevels in
three broad categories: Novice (Low, Mid, High), Intermediate
(Low, Mid, High), and Advanced (Low, Mid, High) (Avant
Assessment,2024). AvantAssessmentdoesnotprovideadirect
alignment between STAMP levels and CEFR levels. However,
we can indirectly use ACTFL’s alignment to CEFR (ACTFL,
n.d.). According to this, for an A2-level proficiency, learners
needtoscoreatIntermediateMidinreceptiveskills(i.e.,reading
and listening) and Intermediate Low in productive skills (i.e.,
writingandspeaking).
For the STAMP 4S test, individual STAMP level ratings were
assigned for reading, listening, writing, and speaking skills,
as well as overall proficiency, which was calculated as the
average of the four skill ratings. If a participant received NR
on speaking or writing, we calculated their overall proficiency
